Leonardo and Michelangelo

Leonardo da Vinci was born in 1452 near Florence, Italy. As a teenager he learned to paint in the workshop of the artist Verrocchio. He was curious about everything: plants, water, birds and the human body.

Leonardo painted The Last Supper on a wall in Milan and the Mona Lisa, a portrait of Lisa del Giocondo. He blended colors softly, with no hard lines. This is called sfumato.

Leonardo kept notebooks with thousands of pages. He sketched ideas for flying machines, a tank and weapons. He also studied anatomy, the parts of the body. He wrote backwards, in mirror writing.

Michelangelo Buonarroti was born in 1475. As a boy he studied art in Florence and lived in the home of the rich Medici family, who were patrons (supporters) of artists.

Michelangelo called himself a sculptor, not a painter. He carved the Pietà and the giant statue David from marble. Pope Julius II hired him to paint the Sistine Chapel ceiling. They argued about it, but he finished in 1512.

Both artists were influenced by the Catholic Church and by Greek and Roman ideas. Both were skilled in many subjects, so each is called a Renaissance man.
