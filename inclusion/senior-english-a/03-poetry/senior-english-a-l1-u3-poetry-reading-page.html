🪪 Day 2: Identity: Autobiographical & Acrostic Poems

📜 River, Not Pond — our team
I would rather be a river than a pond.A river keeps going.
Original example poem written for this class site (stand-in for a copyrighted poem used in class). In class: “Identity” by Julio Noboa Polanco (title only, copyrighted).

They wait by the door.They are too small now.I keep them anyway.
Original example poem written for this class site (stand-in for a copyrighted poem used in class). In class: “Perhaps the World Ends Here” by Joy Harjo (title only, copyrighted).

🎩 Day 4: Limericks: Rhyme, Rhythm & Humor

📜 There was an Old Man with a beard — Edward Lear
There was an Old Man with a beard,Who said, “It is just as I feared!—Two Owls and a Hen,Four Larks and a Wren,Have all built their nests in my beard!”
Edward Lear, A Book of Nonsense (1846). Public domain.

Samuel Taylor Coleridge (1798). Public domain.

🌉 Day 7: Sonnets: Wordsworth & Lazarus

📜 Composed upon Westminster Bridge, September 3, 1802 — William Wordsworth
Earth has not anything to show more fair:
The river glideth at his own sweet will:Dear God! the very houses seem asleep;And all that mighty heart is lying still!
William Wordsworth (1802). Public domain. The class also reads Emma Lazarus, “The New Colossus” (1883, public domain); see the reading packet.

🔁 Day 8: Sestinas: Repetition & Structure

📜 Rain Again (first two stanzas) — our team
The bus is late again. I count the rain.I think about homeand the yellow kitchen light.
Original example poem written for this class site (stand-in for a copyrighted poem used in class). End words: rain, time, road, door, home, light. In class: “The Shrinking Lonesome Sestina” by Miller Williams (title only, copyrighted).

🎨 Day 9: Catch-Up Day: Revise & Illustrate

📜 Revision example — our team
Before: The dog was nice and happy.
After: The dog's tail thumps the floorlike a drum when I come home.
Original example written for this class site.

🌬️ Day 11: Personification: Giving Life to Things

📜 The Wind (first four lines) — James Stephens
The wind stood up and gave a shout.He whistled on his fingers andKicked the withered leaves about
James Stephens, from “The Wind” (1915). Public domain. The class's second poem, “The Little Corn Flakes,” is named by title only (copyrighted); see our toaster stand-in in the reading packet.

🍂 Day 13: Odes: Praise & Playful Exaggeration

📜 Ode to the West Wind (excerpt) — Percy Bysshe Shelley
O wild West Wind, thou breath of Autumn's being,
Wild Spirit, which art moving everywhere;Destroyer and preserver; hear, oh hear!
Percy Bysshe Shelley (1820). Public domain. Also in class: Thomas Gray's cat ode (1748, public domain; see the reading packet). “Ode to Autocorrect” by Martha Silano is named by title only (copyrighted); our mock-ode stand-in is in the reading packet.

🧐 Day 17: Final Part 2: Read, Analyze & Draft

📜 Who Has Seen the Wind? — Christina Rossetti
Who has seen the wind?Neither I nor you:But when the leaves hang trembling,The wind is passing through.
Who has seen the wind?Neither you nor I:But when the trees bow down their heads,The wind is passing by.
Christina Rossetti, Sing-Song (1872). Public domain. Practice poem only; the real final is the teacher's.
